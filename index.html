Why climb

Climb to work,
not just to reach.

Damaged structures, bridges and utility poles need inspection and maintenance on rough surfaces nobody prepared for a robot, and much of that work needs contact, not just a camera. A humanoid already walks and works with its hands on the ground. We want the same body to carry that work up the wall.

Spine palm

Every spine
on its own spring.

A spine catches on a tiny bump when it is dragged the right way. Real walls are irregular, so not every spine finds one at once. Each spine rides on its own spring and moves on its own, so more of them end up in contact.1

Hardware · bench test

A Rizon arm presses one unit onto a cinder block under task-space force control. After a 10 N normal preload, the lateral and shear forces ramp to a test point. The normal force then ramps down through zero and into tension until the unit lets go.

With enough drag along the spines, the unit keeps holding even while it is pulled away from the surface.

Twelve units. Every way they can fail.

The palm holds as long as enough units do. The model tracks which units still hold, how the load is shared among them, and every order in which they can let go.

Which units hold

\[\begin{gathered}\mathbf E=(E_1,\dots,E_N)\\ E_i=1\ \text{effective},\quad E_i=0\ \text{failed}\end{gathered}\]

A palm has \(N=12\) units. We assume their failures are independent and identically distributed.

How the load is shared

\[\begin{gathered}\mathbf w=(F_x,F_y,F_z,M_x,M_y,M_z)^{\top}\\ \mathbf f_i=A_i(\mathbf e)\,\mathbf w\end{gathered}\]

The palm's 6D wrench is split into a force on each unit. The split depends on which units still hold, so it changes whenever one fails.

Preload

\[\begin{aligned}p_0(\mathbf e)&=\Pr\big(\mathbf E^{(0)}=\mathbf e\big)\\&=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-\beta_i)^{e_i}\,\beta_i^{\,1-e_i}\end{aligned}\]

On a random surface some units never engage. Each unit's preload failure is a Bernoulli trial with probability \(\beta_i\).

Fail, share again, repeat

\[m_B=m\prod_{i\in B}q_i\prod_{j\in S\setminus B}r_j\]

From a branch with mass \(m\) and survivors \(S\), subset \(B\) fails in the next round with this mass, where \(q_i\) and \(r_j\) are failure and survival probabilities under the current load. The branches form a tree whose total mass is 1.

Sum the tree

\[\begin{gathered}\pi(\mathbf e;\mathbf w)=\sum_{b\,:\,b\ \text{terminates at}\ \mathbf e} m_b\\ P_{\text{pad fail}}(\mathbf w)=\sum_{\mathbf e\in\mathcal D}\pi(\mathbf e;\mathbf w)\end{gathered}\]

Branches that end in the same final state add up to its probability. Summing over the failed final states \(\mathcal D\) gives the chance that the whole palm lets go.

Fast enough to train with

\[\begin{aligned}\pi(\mathbf e;\mathbf w)&=\sum_{\mathbf e_0}p_0(\mathbf e_0)\\&\quad\cdot\int_{[0,1]^N}\mathbf 1\big[T(\mathbf w,\mathbf e_0,\mathbf u)=\mathbf e\big]\,d\mathbf u\end{aligned}\]

We estimate \(\pi\) by Monte Carlo sampling and store it as an offline dataset, so the GPU training pipeline only has to look it up.

Training

Learn with privileges.
Deploy without them.

A teacher policy learns to climb while seeing what only a simulator knows: contact forces and which spines are engaged. A student then learns to act from proprioception alone.

Stage II

  1. Proprioceptive history \(H_t\)The last 50 frames of joint and body state.
  2. Estimator \(\varphi\)Infers contact force and spine engagement \(\hat e_t\). Trained against the simulator's true values.
  3. Deploy policy \(\pi\)Starts from the teacher's weights. Trained on a fixed 90° wall with domain randomization.
\[\mathcal L(\pi)=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{RL}}(\pi)+\lambda\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi\,\|\,\pi_T\big)\] \(\lambda\) starts large, so the student first imitates the frozen teacher, then shrinks, so the task reward takes over.2

Two rewards for spines.

Spines hold only when they engage on time and with enough preload. Two terms shape that.

Schedule mismatch discount

When a limb's engagement disagrees with its engage gate for longer than a grace period, every tracking reward is discounted, down to a quarter.

\[\begin{gathered}m_h=\mathbf 1[\hat e_h\ne e_h]\cdot\min\!\Big(\frac{d_h\,T_{\mathrm{cyc}}}{\tau_g},\,1\Big)\\ G=f+(1-f)\Big(1-\frac1H\sum_h m_h\Big)\in[0.25,\,1]\end{gathered}\]

with \(\tau_g=0.25\) s and \(f=0.25\). Joint-position, end-effector and root-orientation tracking are multiplied by \(G\):

\[\begin{aligned} r_{\mathrm{jpos}}&=\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\,\mathrm{mean}_j(\hat q_j-q_j)^2\big)\cdot G\\ r_{\mathrm{jvel}}&=\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\,\mathrm{mean}_j(\hat{\dot q}_j-\dot q_j)^2\big)\\ r_{\mathrm{ee}}&=\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\,\mathrm{mean}_h\lVert\hat{\mathbf p}^r_h-\mathbf p^r_h\rVert^2\big)\cdot G\\ r_{\mathrm{ori}}&=\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\,\theta_{\mathrm{geo}}(\hat q^{\mathrm{root}},q^{\mathrm{root}})^2\big)\cdot G \end{aligned}\]

with \(\sigma\) = 0.2 rad, 1.5 rad/s, 0.1 m and 0.3 rad, in order.

In development

References from a task description.

Today's teacher tracks IK-generated cycles. We are building a pipeline that turns a task described in language into a reference the robot can actually execute.

  1. LLM and task adapterLanguage and files become a reference problem: the scene (robot, objects, CAD surfaces), a contact plan (phases, contact modes), and the goal and style of the interaction in the object's frame.
  2. Initial stateWhole-body IK and a static-hold force/torque check.
  3. Event-driven whole-body dynamicsA phase machine drives OSC QP steps; integration returns state and contact events to it.
  4. Trajectory certificateCertified references go on to training. Rejected ones go back to the LLM.

Questions? Answers.

Has the robot climbed a real wall yet?

Not yet. All climbing so far is in simulation. On hardware we have measured single spine units, brought up the full deployment stack, transferred a first ground task, and built stucco and wood test walls.

Climbing the real walls is the next milestone. This page will be updated when it happens.

Why micro-spines?

They grip rough, unprepared surfaces such as concrete and stucco, the kind found on real structures. Each spine hooks a small asperity when it is dragged in the right direction, so the wall needs no preparation.

Why a humanoid, and not a specialized climber?

We do not claim that a humanoid beats a specialized climber on a uniform wall. Its advantage is in mixed missions that combine walking, climbing and working with its hands, in one body.

A general-purpose humanoid is also a good starting point for co-design: it shows what a task really needs from a body, what to remove and what to add.

Does the robot need force sensing?

The deploy policy does not. An estimator infers contact force and spine engagement from the last 50 frames of proprioception. In simulation it is trained against the true values, which only the teacher gets to see.

How is spine contact simulated?

Each palm is twelve units that fail independently. Given the palm's wrench, the load is split among the units that still hold; when some fail, the rest share it again, round by round.

Summing the probability of every failure path gives the chance that the whole palm lets go. We precompute it by Monte Carlo sampling, so it fits a GPU training pipeline.

Where do the reference motions come from?

The current teacher tracks IK-generated climbing cycles with an engage schedule for each hand and foot. We are also building a pipeline that turns a task described in language into a reference, checked by a trajectory certificate before training.
