Overview

Climb to work,
not just to reach.

Damaged structures, bridges and utility poles need inspection and maintenance on rough surfaces nobody prepared for a robot. A humanoid already walks and works with its hands on the ground; we want the same body on the wall. Micro-spines grip such surfaces, but they hold only when loaded the right way, and they let go at random. Everything below is built around that fact.

Contribution 1 · Reference generation

References the
spines can hold.

A spine palm latches only after enough preload, holds only while each unit stays inside its measured limits, and lets go when the load says so. A kinematic reference cannot know when any of that happens. We generate each reference as a whole-body dynamic trajectory under the training simulator's own contact rules, then certify it from the stored trajectory alone.

A task says what. The generator decides how.

A task contributes only an adapter. Generation, the event rules and the certificate are the same code for every task, and the same problem always gives the same bytes.

  1. Task adapterThe scene (robot, objects, surfaces, pad layout), design variables \(\psi\), a contact plan of phases with their exits, interaction relations in the related object's frame, and the task's own checks.
  2. Initial stateWhole-body IK onto the contact targets, free passive joints settled by springs, stops and gravity, and a static hold QP.
  3. Event-driven whole-body dynamicsA QP over joint torques and contact forces every 2 ms. Every 20 ms a referee applies the plant's rules: latches, releases, touchdowns.
  4. CertificateTen checks recomputed from the stored trajectory, and each pad's reliability over 65,536 unit realizations.

When a step is infeasible or a check fails, the generator reports the time, the phase, the conflicting constraints and the shortfall. The design variables or the plan are revised, and generation resumes from the last phase checkpoint. Upstream and optional, a GPU statics search chooses the pad design and a chain of whole-body static keyframes.

Joint torques only. No motor, PD or servo model.

Each integration step solves one QP; each policy step lets the plant decide what changed.

Equations of motion, exactly

\[M(q)\,\dot v+c(q,v)-f_p=S^{\top}\tau+G\,\lambda+R\,r\]

The unknowns are the joint torques \(\tau\), the contact forces \(\lambda\) and the reactions \(r\) of passive joints held on their stops. The accelerations \(\dot v\) are eliminated through this equation, so it holds at every step.

Held contacts and limits

\[\begin{gathered}J\dot v=-\dot J v+k_p e-k_d\dot e\\ |\tau|\le 0.7\,\tau_{\max},\qquad \lambda\in\mathcal K\end{gathered}\]

\(\mathcal K\) is the friction cones and, for a latched spine palm, every unit's load inside its measured domain and a trust box around the current load.

Cost: the task, and the chance to hold

\[\min_{\tau,\lambda,r}\ \sum_k w_k\lVert \mathrm{err}_k\rVert^2+\text{force ramps}-w\log J(\lambda)\]

\(J\) is the probability that every latched palm keeps holding. Each solve is checked against its KKT conditions, or the step fails.

A holding probability the solver can climb

\[J(\lambda)=\prod_{p}\ \frac1{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tilde h\big(\lambda_p;\,u^{(k)}\big),\qquad K=512\]

The plant's failure cascade is run on 512 fixed draws of unit strength, with each of its rules softened so the result is smooth in the load. Near the current load it enters the QP as a quadratic model.

Integrate

\[q^{+},\,v^{+}\ \text{at constant}\ \dot v,\quad h=2\ \text{ms}\]

Contact drift is removed by a mass-weighted projection, and a passive joint that reaches its stop takes an inelastic impact. Every step stores \(q, v, \dot v, \tau, \lambda\), contact points, modes and events.

An event referee every 20 ms

\[\text{preload gate}\xrightarrow{\ \text{debounce}\ }\text{latched}\xrightarrow{\ \ge 99.9\%\ \text{let go}\ }\text{released}\]

4,096 persistent realizations of a latched palm's units follow it through holding windows, early failures and cascades. Rigid parts touch down and lift off. Events end phases, so the plan never schedules a contact change that only the plant may decide.

Trust the trajectory, not the generator.

The certificate reads only the stored trajectory and recomputes every claim with its own code.

Reliability and release

A legal release unhooks. The palm is pulled off along the normal during its unloading phase, while every unit still holding carries at most 0.2 N of shear along the spines. Pulling off under shear is a failure, not a release.

R1, per latch. Each of 65,536 realizations draws its unit strengths once and follows the stored loads through the plant's holding windows, failures and load sharing. It reports the share that hold through the phase, and the share that release legally.

R2, the whole task. Realizations that release legally are grouped by release step; each group resumes the generator from the unloading checkpoint and is certified again. Intervals are Clopper–Pearson.

Contribution 2 · Micro-spine model

From one unit
to a whole palm.

We measured how a single spine unit lets go, then modeled how a twelve-unit palm fails as load shifts between its units. The model runs inside the training simulator, the reference generator and the certificate.

Measured on the bench.

Hardware · bench test

A Rizon arm presses one unit onto a cinder block under task-space force control. After a 10 N normal preload, the lateral and shear forces ramp to a test point. The normal force then ramps down through zero and into tension until the unit lets go.

With enough drag along the spines, the unit keeps holding even while it is pulled away from the surface.

Twelve units. Every way they can fail.

The palm holds as long as enough units do. The model tracks which units still hold, how the load is shared among them, and every order in which they can let go.

Which units hold

\[\begin{gathered}\mathbf E=(E_1,\dots,E_N)\\ E_i=1\ \text{effective},\quad E_i=0\ \text{failed}\end{gathered}\]

A palm has \(N=12\) units. We assume their failures are independent and identically distributed.

How the load is shared

\[\begin{gathered}\mathbf w=(F_x,F_y,F_z,M_x,M_y,M_z)^{\top}\\ \mathbf f_i=A_i(\mathbf e)\,\mathbf w\end{gathered}\]

The palm's 6D wrench is split into a force on each unit. The split depends on which units still hold, so it changes whenever one fails.

Preload

\[\begin{aligned}p_0(\mathbf e)&=\Pr\big(\mathbf E^{(0)}=\mathbf e\big)\\&=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-\beta_i)^{e_i}\,\beta_i^{\,1-e_i}\end{aligned}\]

On a random surface some units never engage. Each unit's preload failure is a Bernoulli trial with probability \(\beta_i\).

Fail, share again, repeat

\[m_B=m\prod_{i\in B}q_i\prod_{j\in S\setminus B}r_j\]

From a branch with mass \(m\) and survivors \(S\), subset \(B\) fails in the next round with this mass, where \(q_i\) and \(r_j\) are failure and survival probabilities under the current load. The branches form a tree whose total mass is 1.

Sum the tree

\[\begin{gathered}\pi(\mathbf e;\mathbf w)=\sum_{b\,:\,b\ \text{terminates at}\ \mathbf e} m_b\\ P_{\text{pad fail}}(\mathbf w)=\sum_{\mathbf e\in\mathcal D}\pi(\mathbf e;\mathbf w)\end{gathered}\]

Branches that end in the same final state add up to its probability. Summing over the failed final states \(\mathcal D\) gives the chance that the whole palm lets go.

Fast enough to train with

\[\begin{aligned}\pi(\mathbf e;\mathbf w)&=\sum_{\mathbf e_0}p_0(\mathbf e_0)\\&\quad\cdot\int_{[0,1]^N}\mathbf 1\big[T(\mathbf w,\mathbf e_0,\mathbf u)=\mathbf e\big]\,d\mathbf u\end{aligned}\]

The simulator samples this integral: each latch draws the units' strengths \(\mathbf u\) once, and one GPU thread follows each palm through every failure round. Only the load-independent geometry of all \(2^{12}=4096\) surviving subsets is precomputed.

Contribution 3 · Hardware design

Every spine
on its own spring.

A spine catches on a tiny bump when it is dragged the right way. Real walls are irregular, so not every spine finds one at once. Each spine rides on its own spring and moves on its own, so more of them end up in contact.1

Assembly

Where the palm hinges decides whether it holds.

The palm turns freely on the limb, so the limb passes only a force through the hinge. A hinge above the spine face turns shear into a peeling moment that lifts the top row; an offset along the face does the same with the normal force.

A fork palm for the 27-DoF wrist

Two columns of six units clamp the wrist roll axis, with the palm centre level with it. A pin hangs the palm in a U-fork: it tilts ±6° on its stops and slides 2 mm along the normal on preloaded springs.

Designed under hard limits on what can be built: hinge geometry, stop angles and spring rates come from the parts. In fourteen corner load cases the forearm's three 9 N·m motors stay within their limits; a rigid flange mount exceeds them.

Stage II

One student, PPO with DAgger

  1. ObservationsProprioceptive history, the wall pose from motion capture, and the skill the operator selects.
  2. Student \(\pi_S\)One policy for every skill. Whether a palm is latched is inferred from proprioception.
  3. Teachers \(\pi_k\)Label the states the student visits with the active skill's teacher action.
\[\mathcal L(\pi_S)=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{PPO}}(\pi_S)+\lambda_D\,\mathbb E_{s\sim\pi_S}\big\lVert \pi_S(o)-\pi_{k}(s)\big\rVert^2\] \(\lambda_D\) falls from 1 to 0.1 over the first half of training, and the imitation term is off wherever the student has left the teacher's own termination bounds. The PPO term stays: imitation alone under-learns short, high-torque phases such as a pull-up.

Two rewards for spines.

Used by the current wall-climbing teacher. Spines hold only when they engage on time and with enough preload. Two terms shape that.

Schedule mismatch discount

When a limb's engagement disagrees with its engage gate for longer than a grace period, every tracking reward is discounted, down to a quarter.

\[\begin{gathered}m_h=\mathbf 1[\hat e_h\ne e_h]\cdot\min\!\Big(\frac{d_h\,T_{\mathrm{cyc}}}{\tau_g},\,1\Big)\\ G=f+(1-f)\Big(1-\frac1H\sum_h m_h\Big)\in[0.25,\,1]\end{gathered}\]

with \(\tau_g=0.25\) s and \(f=0.25\). Joint-position, end-effector and root-orientation tracking are multiplied by \(G\):

\[\begin{aligned} r_{\mathrm{jpos}}&=\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\,\mathrm{mean}_j(\hat q_j-q_j)^2\big)\cdot G\\ r_{\mathrm{jvel}}&=\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\,\mathrm{mean}_j(\hat{\dot q}_j-\dot q_j)^2\big)\\ r_{\mathrm{ee}}&=\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\,\mathrm{mean}_h\lVert\hat{\mathbf p}^r_h-\mathbf p^r_h\rVert^2\big)\cdot G\\ r_{\mathrm{ori}}&=\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\,\theta_{\mathrm{geo}}(\hat q^{\mathrm{root}},q^{\mathrm{root}})^2\big)\cdot G \end{aligned}\]

with \(\sigma\) = 0.2 rad, 1.5 rad/s, 0.1 m and 0.3 rad, in order.

Questions? Answers.

Has the robot climbed a real wall yet?

Not yet. All climbing so far is in simulation. On hardware we have measured single spine units, brought up the full deployment stack, transferred a first ground task, and built stucco and wood test walls.

Climbing the real walls is the next milestone. This page will be updated when it happens.

Why not just retarget human climbing motion?

A human hand and a spine palm hold in different ways. A spine palm needs preload before it latches, holds only while every unit stays inside its limits, and has to be unloaded before it is pulled off. A kinematic motion says nothing about any of that, so a teacher tracking it learns to fight its own contacts.

The generator plans the forces with the motion, and lets the contact model decide when each latch and release happens.

Why micro-spines?

They grip rough, unprepared surfaces such as concrete and stucco, the kind found on real structures. Each spine hooks a small asperity when it is dragged in the right direction, so the wall needs no preparation.

Why a humanoid, and not a specialized climber?

We do not claim that a humanoid beats a specialized climber on a uniform wall. Its advantage is in mixed missions that combine walking, climbing and working with its hands, in one body.

A general-purpose humanoid is also a good starting point for co-design: it shows what a task really needs from a body, what to remove and what to add.

Does the robot need force sensing?

The deploy policy does not. Only the teachers' critics see latch states, unit strengths and contact forces. The student infers whether a palm is latched from its proprioceptive history.

How is spine contact simulated?

Each palm is twelve units that fail independently. Given the palm's wrench, the load is split among the units that still hold; when some fail, the rest share it again, round by round.

In the simulator each latch draws its units' strengths once, and one GPU thread follows each palm through every failure round. The generator and the certificate use the same rules.

What does a certificate guarantee?

That the stored trajectory obeys the robot's dynamics, its limits and the contact model's event rules, checked by code independent of the generator. It says nothing about control: whether a policy can track the reference is what the teachers find out.
